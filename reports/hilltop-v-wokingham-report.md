## Source Reconciliation

Four points where the live audio and the written accounts diverge. Each is recorded here rather than resolved silently.

**1. Who won the penalty.** The live note describes a player "bursting forward again, likes to get forward a lot", through into the box and fouled, and the transcript renders the number as 11. The journalist identifies the player as Sammim Rahmani (#18), the left-back, after a one-two with Benabbas. The journalist's account is adopted: it matches the audio's own description of a full-back breaking forward, matches the first-half pattern of Rahmani doing exactly that twice, and 18/11 is a documented failure mode of the transcription on this audio. An earlier draft attributed this to Kikambi (#11); that attribution was wrong.

**2. How the second goal was built.** The live note reads it as a corner "not cleared very well". The journalist describes a deliberate short-corner routine — played short, a turn on the edge of the box, a lofted delivery. These are not the same event description. The journalist's version is adopted as the build-up, with the audio's read retained for the finish, where a parried effort and a scrambled follow-up is consistent with what "not cleared very well" would look like from the touchline. The distinction matters: a rehearsed routine that pulls the danger outside the box is a different defensive problem from a failure to clear.

**3. The opposition press.** The first-half audio is unambiguous that Wokingham did not press. The opening goal came from a press. Both can be true — the audio covers roughly the first twelve minutes of the half in detail and almost nothing between then and 58'. There is no material available to establish whether this was a half-time adjustment or an isolated event, and it is left open.

**4. The negative first-touch note.** The transcript renders "the ball goes in for number 19, he's only ever got his back to the defender, his first touch is always taking him backwards". The described role is a centre-forward receiving to feet with his back to goal, which is Kahssay (#9), not Mahdi (#19) at right-back; the journalist independently praises Mahdi's performance. The note has been reassigned to #9, where it also matches the separate hold-up-play observations. 9/19 is a documented failure mode of the transcription on this audio.

## Data Quality Note

The audio is the limiting factor on everything tactical in this report. It was recorded at roughly -36 dBFS with continuous wind and crowd noise, consistent with a phone in a pocket rather than held or clipped. Recovery required gain normalisation and high-pass filtering, then two independent Whisper passes (large-v3 and turbo) merged window-by-window because each recovered speech the other lost. Passages where both passes failed are absent from this report rather than guessed at.

Three limitations apply. First, coverage: 17 minutes 29 seconds of notes across a 90-minute match, weighted towards the opening of the first half and the closing half-hour. The period between roughly 12' and 58' is almost entirely unobserved, which is why the first-half narrative here is structural rather than chronological. Second, spoken shirt numbers are the least reliable element of the transcript — 8/18, 9/19, 11/14 and 20/2 are all confused by the transcription on this audio, and two of the four reconciliations above are number errors. Every number spoken does resolve to a real player in the squad, and the 66'/67' double substitution the audio conflated into one was independently confirmed, which is the basis of the roster check passing; but individual numbers carry no independent weight without corroboration. Third, the audio carries no minute markers, so phase timings are anchored to the goal and substitution events the notes describe rather than measured.

The 4-2-1-3 and its positional assignments are the club's own shape as confirmed by the coach, and the audio walk-through is consistent with it [A].
